I am an undergraduate student in Data Science at New York University (CAS '27). My research interests lie at the intersection of out-of-distribution generalization, high-dimensional statistics, and machine learning for empirical social and cognitive sciences.

At NYU, I conduct research in Fox Lab with Dr. Pascal Wallisch studying cognitive pattern recognition and music perception, and in Dr. Laurence Maloney's lab investigating the mathematical geometry of auditory timbre perception. Previously, I worked on empirical econometric policy modeling at the University of Wisconsin–Madison's Center for Research on the Wisconsin Economy (CROWE) with Dr. Ananth Seshadri.

Outside of mathematics and statistics, I am an upright and electric bassist performing with the NYU All-University Jazz Orchestra and NYU Orchestra, and a music educator.

Research Interests Focus Areas

• Out-of-Distribution Generalization

Statistical foundations of domain adaptation, covariate and concept shift, invariant representations, and robustness when test distributions diverge from training environments.

• High-Dimensional Statistics

Concentration of measure, high-dimensional estimation, regularized empirical risk minimization, and theoretical performance bounds for modern machine learning algorithms.

• Causal Inference & Econometrics

Difference-in-differences, quasi-experimental methods, observational causal discovery, and identification strategies applied to public policy and socio-economic dynamics.

• ML for Cognitive & Social Science

Probabilistic modeling of human perceptual geometry (e.g. timbre space), behavioral decision-making, and empirical psychophysics.

Mapping Timbre Space: Can We Make More Beautiful Instruments? NYU DURF Grant Proposal
Advisor: Dr. Laurence Maloney • Department of Psychology & Center for Neural Science

Investigates the high-dimensional geometric structure of musical timbre and human auditory preference. Proposes an empirical paradigm using scalable harmonic audio transformations, non-linear neural networks to predict perceptual deviation from uncorrupted audio, gradient-based latent optimization to identify optimal timbres, and non-linear dimensionality reduction (t-SNE) to uncover topological manifold properties of timbre space.

Why do People Listen to Sad Music? Awarded $1,250 Grant
Advisor: Dr. Pascal Wallisch • Department of Data Science & Neural Science (Fox Lab)

Formulates a quantitative solution to the "sad music affinity paradox" by evaluating the multi-way interactions among transient mood states, psychometric personality dimensions (TIPI, Dirty Dozen), depression indicators (PHQ-9), familiarity, and acoustic features. Employs binary logistic regression classification on bifurcated valence responses and signal processing feature extraction with Librosa in Python.
